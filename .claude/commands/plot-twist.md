description: Generate plot twists, complications, and unexpected developments for crime fiction stories
---

You are now helping the user develop plot twists and complications for their crime fiction story.

## Your Task

Brainstorm and develop surprising but logical plot twists that enhance the mystery. Follow this process:

### Step 1: Understand the Story Context

Ask the user:

1. **Current story state**:
   - Do they have an outline? (Check `outlines/` directory)
   - What's the basic premise?
   - Who are the main characters?
   - What's the central crime?

2. **Where in the story?**
   - Opening hook?
   - First act complication?
   - Midpoint twist?
   - Third act revelation?
   - Final twist ending?

3. **What type of twist?**
   - **Suspect twist**: Wrong person under suspicion
   - **Victim twist**: Victim isn't who we thought
   - **Motive twist**: Reason for crime is different than assumed
   - **Method twist**: Crime committed differently than believed
   - **Ally twist**: Trusted person is involved
   - **Timeline twist**: Events didn't happen when we thought
   - **Identity twist**: Someone isn't who they claim
   - **Double crime twist**: More than one crime occurred
   - **Reversal twist**: Protagonist's assumption completely wrong
   - **Something else?**

### Step 2: Assess Current Elements

Gather information about:

1. **What's already established?**
   - Key facts that must remain true
   - Characters and their roles
   - Timeline and events
   - Clues already planted

2. **What assumptions have been created?**
   - What do readers likely believe?
   - What has protagonist concluded?
   - What seems certain but isn't confirmed?

3. **Where's the story feeling predictable?**
   - What's too obvious?
   - Where could surprise fit?
   - What would readers not expect?

### Step 3: Define Goals for the Twist

Ask what the twist should accomplish:

1. **Purpose**:
   - [ ] Raise stakes
   - [ ] Change direction of investigation
   - [ ] Deepen mystery
   - [ ] Create new danger
   - [ ] Reveal character
   - [ ] Reframe previous events
   - [ ] Add emotional complexity
   - [ ] Provide satisfaction (final twist)

2. **Tone**:
   - Dark and shocking?
   - Clever and satisfying?
   - Emotionally devastating?
   - Game-changing?

3. **Reader reaction desired**:
   - "I didn't see that coming!"
   - "Of course! The clues were there!"
   - "Everything is different now!"
   - "I need to reread to catch what I missed!"

### Step 4: Check Constraints

Clarify any limits:

- What elements are non-negotiable?
- Are there any clichés to avoid?
- Should this be the main twist or a complication?
- Does it need to be fully resolved or can some mystery remain?
- Any genre-specific requirements (cozy mysteries avoid gore, etc.)?

### Step 5: Brainstorm Multiple Options

Generate 3-5 different twist possibilities, each including:

1. **The twist itself**: What's revealed or happens
2. **Setup required**: What clues/details need to be planted earlier
3. **Impact**: How it changes the story
4. **Feasibility**: How well it fits existing story elements
5. **Fair play**: Can readers solve it with available information?

For each option, consider:
- **Logical consistency**: Does it make sense with established facts?
- **Character motivation**: Do people act for believable reasons?
- **Foreshadowing**: Can it be set up subtly?
- **Payoff**: Is the revelation satisfying?
- **Consequences**: How does it affect the rest of the story?

### Step 6: Present Options

Show the user the twist options with:

**For each twist**:
```
## Twist Option [Number]: [Catchy name]

**The Reveal**: [What happens / what's revealed]

**How It Works**: [Explanation of the twist]

**Setup Needed**:
- [Clue or detail to plant in chapter X]
- [Character behavior to establish early]
- [Timeline detail to mention]

**Impact on Story**:
- Changes: [What's different after this reveal]
- New questions: [What readers will now wonder]
- Stakes: [How danger/urgency changes]

**Pros**:
- [Why this works well]
- [What makes it surprising yet fair]

**Cons**:
- [Potential challenges]
- [What might need to change]

**Fair Play Rating**: [Can attentive readers solve this? Yes/Partially/No]
```

### Step 7: Refine Chosen Twist

Once the user selects a twist (or elements from multiple):

1. **Develop the details**:
   - Exact mechanics of how it works
   - Timeline of actual events
   - Character motivations and actions
   - Evidence that exists

2. **Plan the foreshadowing**:
   - What clues to plant (and where)
   - How to make them visible but not obvious
   - Red herrings to misdirect
   - Character behaviors that make sense in retrospect

3. **Map the revelation**:
   - When does the twist occur?
   - How is it revealed?
   - Who figures it out?
   - How do characters react?

4. **Check logic**:
   - Walk through the timeline with the twist
   - Verify all character actions make sense
   - Ensure physical evidence supports it
   - Confirm alibis work (or deliberately don't)

### Step 8: Integration Planning

Help the user integrate the twist:

1. **Update documents**:
   - Revise outline in `outlines/` to include twist
   - Update `timeline.md` with actual events
   - Modify `clues.md` to include foreshadowing

2. **Identify scenes to add/modify**:
   - Where to plant clues
   - What character moments to adjust
   - Which scenes need rewriting

3. **Track implications**:
   - How does this affect the ending?
   - What subplots are impacted?
   - Do any characters' roles change?

## Types of Effective Twists

### Midpoint Twist (50% through)
**Purpose**: Completely change the direction of investigation

**Examples**:
- Supposed victim is actually alive and the killer
- "Murder" was actually suicide framed as murder
- Wrong case entirely—real crime is something else
- Detective's own organization is involved

**Requirements**:
- Raises stakes significantly
- Sends investigation in new direction
- Makes Act 1 events look different in retrospect

### Identity Twist
**Purpose**: Someone isn't who they claim to be

**Examples**:
- Witness is actually the criminal's accomplice
- Victim had a secret identity
- Detective is personally connected (unknowingly)
- Character is using assumed identity

**Requirements**:
- Plausible reason for deception
- Clues to real identity scattered throughout
- Reveal changes understanding of their actions

### Motive Twist
**Purpose**: Crime happened for different reason than believed

**Examples**:
- Apparent robbery was actually murder for revenge
- "Crime of passion" was actually calculated plan
- Money wasn't the motive—it was something personal
- Killer was protecting someone else

**Requirements**:
- New motive is stronger than assumed one
- Explains details that didn't quite fit
- Adds emotional or psychological depth

### Double Layer Twist
**Purpose**: Multiple crimes or criminals

**Examples**:
- Two different people committed two different crimes
- Copycat killer used original murder as cover
- Solving one crime reveals another
- Investigator unknowingly investigating their own crime

**Requirements**:
- Each layer is logical
- Clues exist for both layers
- Combination is more interesting than simple solution

### Timeline Twist
**Purpose**: Events didn't happen when we thought

**Examples**:
- Victim died earlier than discovered
- Killer's alibi is for wrong time (crime happened later)
- Flashbacks reveal truth about past events
- Present-day crime connected to historical event

**Requirements**:
- Timeline manipulation is logical
- Reason for misdirection about timing
- Careful tracking of actual sequence

### Ally Twist
**Purpose**: Trusted person is involved

**Examples**:
- Partner/friend is the killer
- Mentor is corrupt
- Victim's family member is guilty
- Fellow investigator is covering up

**Requirements**:
- Strong motivation for betrayal
- Subtle suspicious behaviors throughout
- Personal emotional impact
- Not obvious or clichéd (avoid "partner did it" unless brilliantly done)

## Criteria for Great Twists

A good twist should be:

1. **Surprising but inevitable**: Shocking when revealed, but obvious in retrospect
2. **Fairly clued**: Attentive readers had the information to solve it
3. **Logical**: Makes sense with character motivations and physics
4. **Meaningful**: Changes our understanding significantly
5. **Emotionally resonant**: Has impact beyond clever mechanics
6. **Necessary**: Improves the story rather than existing just to shock

## Twists to Avoid (Clichés)

- **"It was all a dream"**: Negates the story
- **Evil twin**: Overdone and usually implausible
- **Everyone is guilty**: Dilutes responsibility
- **Random stranger did it**: Character never introduced before
- **Supernatural explanation**: Unless genre supports it
- **Protagonist has split personality**: Rarely done well
- **The actual detective did it**: Very hard to pull off fairly

## Example Opening

Start with something like:

"I'll help you develop an exciting plot twist for your crime fiction story!

First, let me understand where you are in your story development:

1. Do you have an outline or basic premise already? (I can check the `outlines/` directory)
2. What's the central crime or mystery in your story?
3. Where in the story do you want this twist to occur?
   - Early complication?
   - Midpoint revelation that changes everything?
   - Late-story surprise?
   - Final twist ending?

And what kind of twist are you thinking about, or should I suggest various types?"

## After Completion

Once a twist is developed:

1. **Document it properly**:
   - Update outline with the twist
   - Create timeline of actual events
   - List all clues to plant

2. **Suggest next steps**:
   - Use `/scene` to write the revelation scene
   - Use `/character` to deepen character motivations
   - Review existing scenes to add foreshadowing
   - Use `/edit` to revise earlier sections with new information

3. **Remind about fair play**:
   - Ensure clues are present
   - Check that it's solvable
   - Verify timeline works
   - Confirm character motivations hold up

Now begin by engaging with the user about their story and what kind of twist they're looking for!
